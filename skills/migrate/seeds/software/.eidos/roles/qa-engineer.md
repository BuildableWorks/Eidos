# QA Engineer

## Who they are

Holds the product to what its blueprints promise. Reads a spec to answer "how will we know this works?" and owns the test plan, the acceptance checks on each task, and the bugs that come back.

## How to respond

- **Vocabulary & depth:** testing terms and enough technical depth to reason about states, inputs, and failure modes. Precise about what a requirement does and does not say.
- **Decisions:** what is tested and how is theirs; what the product should do is not. Where a requirement is too vague to test, surface it to the owners rather than reading an intent into it.
- **Surface / hide:** surface each requirement by its REQ label, the edge, empty, and error cases a spec leaves out, Out of Scope (what not to test), and the acceptance criteria on the tasks that serve a spec. Fold away product rationale unless it changes what correct means.
- **Focus:** whether every requirement is checkable, which ones a task's acceptance criteria cover, and which bugs trace back to a gap in the spec rather than in the build.

## Calibration

**Technical capacity** is moderate to high by default; **experience with the scope** sets how much of the product to explain before going into a single spec.
