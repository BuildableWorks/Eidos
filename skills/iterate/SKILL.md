# Iterate

One idea, questioned until it holds still. You write no file; the output is a shared understanding the owner can stand behind. A blueprint authored from one line reads as settled while nobody has decided anything.

## Before you ask anything

- **Read `me.md`** and the role contract it names; follow it. Blank means full facilitation; offer `whoami`.
- **Read the framework**: `Framework.yaml` for folders, variants, grouping, Properties, and Vocabulary. Ask in the root's own terms; where the owner uses a near-miss, ask which they mean. No `.eidos/` means offer `install`.
- **Read the neighbors**: the target collection's `index` entries and any adjacent blueprint. Questions asked against what exists are worth ten asked in a vacuum.

## Three passes

Run in order, as a conversation, not a script.

**1. Which template.** Propose the collection and variant, say why in a sentence, and ask. Three answers matter more than a smooth fit: it fits no collection (a top-level doc, or `configure` adds a collection; never force it), it fits two (two ideas wearing one name; offer to split), or it is bigger than a blueprint (name the pieces, pick one for this session). Once placed, read the variant's template; its sections are what you need to know by the end.

**2. What is the intent.** Who is it for and what changes for them? What does it do that nothing here does today? What breaks if it is never made? What will it deliberately not do? Press hardest on non-goals, offering candidates to reject: "so this doesn't cover X?" is easier than an open question.

**3. How it fits.** What must exist first? What does it touch? Where is the seam with the nearest existing blueprint? Does it obsolete or contradict anything written? Which group, and is "a new one" a decision made on purpose?

## How to ask

- Two to four questions at a time, tightest first. Concrete beats open.
- Never answer your own question. Proposing an option to accept or reject is facilitation; recording your guess as their answer is authoring.
- "I don't know" is a result: an open question, written down.
- Notice the words. Two words for one thing, or one no Vocabulary entry declares, is worth asking about; carry it to the recap as a candidate term for `configure`.
- Play back what you heard every few rounds. The corrections are the content.
- Don't run the template as a form.

## When you're done

Stop at the first of: you can state it in three or four sentences the owner agrees with; two rounds add nothing; the owner is out of answers. Close with a recap (placement, intent, non-goals, connections, open questions, candidate terms) and offer `eidos`. Ending without a blueprint is a fine outcome: an idea that turns out to be two, or already covered, or not worth doing, is the cheapest thing this skill produces.
