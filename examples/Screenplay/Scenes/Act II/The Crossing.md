# The Crossing

## Logline

Halfway across the flats, the buyer's party reaches water where the map promised none, and Mira
recognizes her own mark.

## Intent

Turns a private failure into a shared danger. Everything before this is Mira's problem; from here it
is everyone's, and she is the only one who can name the cause.

## Open Questions

- How many are in the party. Three keeps it intimate; five makes the stakes legible. Unresolved.
- Whether the water is a real hazard or only a detour — the film may not need actual peril.

## Beats

- **B1:** The party stops at an edge of standing water that shouldn't be there.
- **B2:** The buyer unrolls the sheet and looks for the waypoint.
- **B3:** The one subjective shot: Mira's point of view, horizon reading correctly, water where it should be. It is not what she sees.
- **B4:** She looks away first.

## The Turn

Mira knows the mark is hers and the audience knows she knows. The film's only remaining question
becomes whether she says so.

## Out of Scope

Does not resolve whether she confesses — that is [What She Says](What%20She%20Says.md). Does not
endanger anyone physically; this film's stakes are reputational, and a rescue would swallow them.
Does not flash back to the marking, per [Voice](../../Frames/Voice.md).

## Dependencies

Requires [The Commission](../Act%20I/The%20Commission.md) — without the mark in its **B3**, the sheet
here is just a wrong map.

## Notes & Decisions

The subjective shot is the film's one broken rule, and it earns the break by lying. Flagged in
[Voice](../../Frames/Voice.md) so nobody removes it as an inconsistency.
