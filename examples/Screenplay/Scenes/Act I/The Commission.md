# The Commission

## Logline

A buyer lays money on the table for a route across the salt flats, and Mira agrees to draw it without
once looking down at the sheet between them.

## Intent

Establishes the transaction the whole film rests on, and shows Mira concealing rather than
discovering. The audience must understand that she knows, and is choosing, before anything goes
wrong — otherwise Act II reads as an accident instead of a debt.

## Open Questions

- Does the buyer know the flats at all? A buyer who has crossed before makes her concealment harder and the scene better, but costs a line of exposition somewhere.
- Whether money is on screen or implied.

## Beats

- **B1:** The buyer spreads a sheet on the table and turns it to face her.
- **B2:** Mira keeps her eyes on him and talks about the route from memory, accurately.
- **B3:** He asks her to mark a waypoint. She marks it without breaking eye contact, and it lands slightly wrong.
- **B4:** He rolls the sheet, satisfied. She watches it go.

## The Turn

The audience learns she cannot read, and learns it from a mark she makes rather than a mark she fails
to make. She is now responsible for something she cannot check.

## Out of Scope

Does not explain the condition, name it, or show its onset — that is nowhere in this film. Does not
establish her reputation; [Cold Open](Cold%20Open.md) already did. Does not reveal that the waypoint
is wrong; [The Crossing](../Act%20II/The%20Crossing.md) carries that.

## Dependencies

The viewer must have seen [Cold Open](Cold%20Open.md), or the mark in **B3** means nothing.

## Notes & Decisions

Single location, two actors, one prop. The cheapest scene in the film and the one that has to work.
