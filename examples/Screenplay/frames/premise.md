# Premise

## The Story

Mira surveyed the salt flats for thirty years and drew the only maps anyone trusted. Now the symbols
on her own sheets have stopped resolving — she can see them, name them, and no longer know what they
point at. A buyer arrives wanting one last route across the flats, and does not know she cannot read
the map he is paying for. She takes the commission and walks it herself.

## Why It Has To Exist

It is about what happens to a person whose competence was their whole identity, told without a
diagnosis, a doctor, or a speech about it. The flats do the explaining: a landscape with no landmarks,
where being lost and being exactly where you meant to be look identical.

## The Shape of It

Two acts. Act I is the commission and the burning of the last sheet she still trusts — she chooses to
go without it rather than be caught not reading it. Act II is the crossing, where the map she sold
turns out to be wrong in a way only she could have caught, and she has to decide whether to say so.

## What It Is Not

Not a mystery about who forged the maps. Not a film that names her condition. Not a redemption — she
does not get the reading back.
