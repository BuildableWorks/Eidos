# Voice

## Tone

Dry and unhurried. Near *The Rider* in its patience with work being done, and near *First Cow* in
letting a transaction carry the emotion. Unlike both, it never cuts to landscape for its own sake —
the flats are a working surface, not scenery.

## Camera & Cut

Mostly waist-up and hands. When Mira reads, we are on the paper before we are on her face, and we
hold a beat past comfortable. The camera never takes her point of view while she is failing to read;
that would explain it, and the film does not explain it.

## Rules It Keeps

- No score. Wind, boot on crust, paper.
- No flashback. Whatever we learn, we learn in the present tense.
- Nobody says the word "lost."
- Every scene has one object in it that Mira handles.

## Rules It Breaks, On Purpose

Once, in **The Crossing**, the camera takes her point of view and the horizon reads correctly. It is
the only subjective shot in the film and it is a lie.
